# Flow and Coherence Review

Flow combines clarity, coherence, and concision. Review the reasoning from the
intended reader's perspective: can they understand the terminology, follow
the sequence, and see how each part advances the section's purpose?

## Reverse outline (反向提纲)

For a substantial draft, or when the argument's structure is unclear:

1. State the paper's central claim or the section's purpose, using only what
   the available material supports.
2. For each paragraph, record its actual main point and the evidence or
   explanation that supports it. Use the topic sentence if it expresses that
   point; otherwise summarize the paragraph as written.
3. Check whether each paragraph has a clear role in the argument, whether its
   supporting details serve that role, and whether the sequence makes sense.
4. Revise, move, combine, or remove material as appropriate. Preserve content
   needed for accuracy or for the author's purpose; a paragraph may be needed
   even if its connection is currently implicit.

For a short excerpt, use a lighter version and identify relationships that
cannot be judged without the surrounding text. If a full reverse outline is
unnecessary, temporary headings or short labels can reveal the structure;
keep headings in the final text only when they suit the document and venue.

## Sentence and paragraph transitions

- Look for missing premises, abrupt topic changes, repeated points, and terms
  that shift meaning. Repair the underlying relationship first.
- Make causal, contrastive, comparative, sequential, or illustrative
  relationships explicit when a reader needs help following them. Choose the
  wording that actually matches the logic.
- Prefer natural connections to strings of formulaic transition words. Do not
  add a transition if the relationship is already clear.

For reviews, report the most consequential issues and their effects on the
reader. When revising, preserve the intended meaning and evidence. If accuracy
requires a substantive change to a claim, flag the issue and explain any
proposed narrowing to the author; do not add unsupported results.
