# Abstract Writing

First plan the argument, then choose a suitable structure and revise it.
Use the structures below as guides, adapting them to the paper, venue, length,
and available evidence.

## Prepare the story

Before drafting, work through these questions:

1. What technical problem does the paper solve, and why do existing approaches
   lack a well-established solution in this setting?
2. What is the technical contribution?
3. What is the underlying reason the approach can work?
4. What technical advantage does it offer, and what **new insight (新认知)**
   does the work provide?

Ground answers in the supplied material. Do not assert that no established
solution exists, or claim a new insight, mechanism, or advantage, unless the
evidence supports it. Distinguish a proposed rationale from an experimentally
established finding. If a critical answer is missing, identify the gap rather
than inventing one.

## Choose a structure

**Insight-led structure (YU's preference when the material supports it):**
task → technical challenge and its cause → key insight → the technical
contribution that realizes it → resulting advantage → representative
experimental evidence.

**Direct contribution structure:** task → technical challenge and its cause →
one or two sentences on the contribution → its advantage → representative
experimental evidence. Use this when a separate insight sentence would be
artificial or less clear.

**Multiple-contribution structure:** task and relevant challenge → each
important contribution paired with its technical advantage → representative
experimental evidence. Select only the contributions needed to explain the
paper; do not turn the abstract into an exhaustive inventory.

## Revise

- Focus the challenge on the problem the paper actually addresses and make its
  connection to the proposed approach clear.
- Name the relevant technique or technical idea in understandable terms;
  bridge it to the challenge so the reader does not encounter an unexplained
  jump. Avoid describing every implementation step.
- State why the contribution matters and include the strongest relevant
  evidence available. Preserve the conditions of comparisons and avoid
  inventing metrics, results, or claims of superiority.
- Revise for a coherent sequence and the venue's constraints. Do not force a
  fixed number of sentences or add a formulaic opening.
