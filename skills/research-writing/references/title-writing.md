# Paper Titles and Method Names

A title signals what the work is about and which research communities may
find it relevant; depending on the venue, that framing may also affect reviewer
selection. Write for the intended audience while accurately representing the
paper's scope and contribution.

## Develop a title

1. Before drafting titles, write down a few important keywords from the actual
   work: the task or problem, technical approach, and any setting or result
   that is essential to its identity.
2. Build a title around the most informative of those terms. It can name the
   task, the problem addressed, and/or the technique used; select the details
   that help the reader understand this paper rather than forcing every term
   into the title.
3. Check that a reader can tell what the work is about and remember its
   distinctive idea. Prefer a specific, meaningful phrase to a generic label,
   and keep the strength and scope of the title faithful to the evidence.

When naming a method, use a phrase that conveys the method's idea or role.
Avoid a catchy but opaque name or an acronym that obscures the technical
content. Offer distinct title angles when useful, and explain the tradeoff
between them if the user is choosing among different audiences or emphases.
