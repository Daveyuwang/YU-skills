# Related Work

Position the paper in relation to the research that matters most to its
problem, method, and contribution. Give close prior work enough attention to
make the relationship to the present paper clear.

## Plan the section

1. Identify the papers most directly related to the task and method first,
   including close alternatives or predecessors that readers need to assess
   the contribution.
2. Choose a small set of meaningful topics from the relevant research
   directions and technical approaches. Group the papers by the ideas or
   problems they address; a paper may belong to more than one group.
3. Organize the discussion around those groups and their relationship to the
   paper's contribution. Include foundational or recent work when it helps
   explain the field, and avoid adding unrelated topics merely to lengthen
   the bibliography.

## Write the comparison

- Explain the representative approaches in each group and the meaningful
  similarities and differences from this work: task or setting, assumptions,
  technique, or objective, as relevant.
- Represent closely related methods fairly, including what they achieve. Make
  distinctions specific and supported rather than claiming novelty merely
  because the methods use different names or implementation details.
- Use the section to deepen the positioning introduced earlier, without
  repeating the introduction or turning the discussion into a paper-by-paper
  list.
- Check that citations and descriptions match their sources. If relevant work
  or its details are unknown, identify the gap and verify when possible; do not
  invent citations or claim the literature search is exhaustive.
