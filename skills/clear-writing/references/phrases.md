# Wording Examples

Use these examples to find filler or formulaic language. Judge each occurrence
in context; do not remove words that carry necessary meaning.

- **Preferred replacements:** Inspect `within` and `across` for a simpler
  equivalent that keeps the original scope and precision.
- **Empty intensifiers:** `really`, `just`, `literally`, `genuinely`,
  `honestly`, `simply`, `actually`, `deeply`, `truly`, `fundamentally`,
  `inherently`, `inevitably`, `interestingly`, `importantly`, and `crucially`.
- **Announcing the point:** `Here's the thing`, `It turns out`, `Let me be
  clear`, `The uncomfortable truth is`, `The truth is`, and `Can we talk
  about`. State the underlying point when the preface adds nothing.
- **Performative emphasis:** `Full stop`, `Let that sink in`, `Make no
  mistake`, and similar prompts to react.
- **Business jargon:** Inspect phrases such as `navigate challenges`, `lean
  into`, `game-changer`, `deep dive`, `moving forward`, and `circle back`.
  Prefer a concrete verb when it expresses the intended action.
- **Meta-commentary:** Inspect `In this section, we'll`, `As we'll see`,
  `Let me walk you through`, `Plot twist`, and similar announcements. Remove
  them when they do not help the reader follow the text.
- **Vague significance:** Replace claims such as `the implications are
  significant` or `the stakes are high` with the specific implication or
  consequence when the source supports it.
