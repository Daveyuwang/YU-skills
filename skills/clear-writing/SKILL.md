# Clear Writing

Write for the reader in concise, readable prose. State the main point early,
name the specific facts, and include the detail needed to understand them.
Adapt the tone and format to the task and audience.

## Punctuation and words

- Do not use an em dash (U+2014) in your own prose. Recast the sentence or use
  a period, comma, colon, or parentheses. Preserve exact quotations, names,
  code, and other text when the task requires faithful reproduction.
- Generally avoid `within` and `across` when a simpler alternative conveys the
  same meaning. Keep the original if replacement would change its scope or
  precision.
- Avoid adverbs, especially empty intensifiers such as `really`, `simply`, and
  `actually`. Remove or rephrase them when the meaning remains intact; retain
  necessary qualifications rather than making an inaccurate claim.
- Replace empty emphasis, vague assertions, and business jargon with concrete
  language. Avoid sweeping words such as `always` or `everyone` unless the
  claim supports them. See [wording examples](references/phrases.md) for a
  focused cleanup.

## Sentences and structure

- State the point directly. Avoid formulaic negation followed by a reveal,
  lists of what something is not, and rhetorical setups that delay the point.
  Keep a real contrast when it is needed to express the facts or the task.
- Prefer active voice and name the responsible actor when known. Avoid vague
  or figurative agency that hides who did what. Do not invent a person, address
  the reader as an actor without reason, or misdescribe a technical process.
- Avoid starting ordinary prose sentences with `What`, `When`, `Where`,
  `Which`, `Who`, `Why`, or `How`; recast around a concrete subject or action.
  Avoid paragraph openings with `So` and sentence openings with `Look,`.
  Retain a direct question or required wording when the task calls for it.
- Use complete, natural sentences. Avoid stacked dramatic fragments, questions
  answered immediately for effect, repeated punchy endings, and three-item
  lists built merely for rhythm. Keep every item that the content requires.

## Preserve meaning

Keep facts, evidence, uncertainty, comparisons, and important qualifications
intact. Do not change a quotation, citation, technical term, code fragment, or
explicitly requested wording just to satisfy a style preference. Preserve units
and inclusive or exclusive bounds when simplifying numerical language. A
direct rewrite should remain faithful to the source and the user's requested
scope.
