# Pipeline and Interaction Diagrams

Start from the actual mechanism, evidence, and figure brief. Identify the
participants, modules, inputs, outputs, decisions, branches, and feedback
only when they exist in the work. Draw the general process clearly; use
specific examples as local explanations rather than substitutes for the
method itself.

## Establish the flow

1. Map the starting point, main path, important dependencies, and any real
   branches or loops. Make the principal flow more prominent than supporting
   inputs, and keep an entity recognizable from stage to stage.
2. Label branch conditions near the relevant exits and show mutually exclusive
   outcomes as alternatives, not simultaneous events. Do not introduce a
   decision module, actor, or edge that the method does not contain.
3. Put participants in a feedback exchange near one another when practical.
   Connect arrows to the actual objects, reduce unnecessary crossings and
   bends, and make labels easy to associate with their own paths.

## Group and connect

- Use light dashed outlines and whitespace to group phases, subsystems, or
  related items when that helps reading. Give group names their own space;
  do not draw a group around every word.
- Within a figure, use dashed boundaries consistently for grouping. Give
  feedback, optional actions, or other relationships distinct and clearly
  labeled connectors. A boundary does not replace a real flow connection.
- Keep repeated objects in consistent order, shape, and identity. Change only
  the parts that represent a real state change and put short explanations
  next to the corresponding objects.

## Preserve the scientific comparison

Use the actual inputs, conditions, and recorded outcomes. If the argument
depends on a shared input, alternative conditions, a baseline, or different
correct outcomes, make those relationships visible instead of removing them
to save space. Generalize module labels where useful, but do not invent or
alter the supporting example. After rendering, check whether the method is
understandable without relying on the example alone.
