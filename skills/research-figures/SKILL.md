# Research Figures

Design professional, clear, restrained figures around the paper's actual
message and evidence. These are YU's defaults for new figures; adapt them to
the brief, existing manuscript, venue, and explicit task instructions.

## Establish the brief

Identify the main message, relevant manuscript or source data, intended
audience, required formats, final display size, and venue constraints. Infer
what can be inferred from the supplied context; flag essential missing
information rather than inventing it. For a revision, preserve the useful
parts of the existing design and change what the task calls for.

When visual references are useful, consider relevant examples from the same
field and other fields. Extract helpful techniques for flow, hierarchy,
grouping, or illustration, then design for the current content; a reference
does not prescribe the layout or set a ceiling on quality.

## Shared design preferences

- **Give the main idea visual priority.** Allocate space by importance rather
  than making every panel equal. Use alignment, spacing, and whitespace to
  make the core method and key relationships easy to find; examples support
  the method rather than becoming the main visual subject.
- **Keep figure text concise.** By default, omit an internal overall title and
  panel letters such as a, b, c. Refer to positions in the caption when that is
  unambiguous. Avoid commas in figure text and keep long explanations in the
  caption. Preserve exact quoted source text and follow venue or explicit
  labeling requirements when needed.
- **Match typography to meaning.** Match general labels and prose to the
  manuscript's font when known. Use a compatible monospaced font for code,
  tool output, filenames, or paths when useful, and appropriate mathematical
  typography for notation consistent with the manuscript. Keep each role
  consistent and do not squeeze content into tiny or excessively condensed
  text.
- **Use color purposefully.** Assign coordinated roles to the main structure,
  important method elements, entities, and supporting material. Keep related
  elements consistent and supporting elements visually quieter. Reinforce
  important distinctions with shape, line style, or labels. Check text against
  its immediate background, including colored fills, and review grayscale and
  color-vision accessibility.
- **Use symbols for their real meaning.** Do not replace natural-language
  phrases with symbols such as `+`, `±`, `×`, `→`, or `=`. Actual mathematics
  and connections in a flow diagram may use the appropriate symbols.
- **Use icons and illustrations selectively.** When emoji are appropriate,
  YU prefers original Apple emoji PNG assets over improvised line-art icons.
  Keep each emoji independently movable and editable as an image component;
  do not pretend an unavailable asset is original. Keep official brand marks
  faithful to their sources. Generate a conceptual illustration when useful,
  but do not use it to invent experimental evidence or distort data.

## Represent the work faithfully

For method or interaction diagrams, use
[pipeline diagrams](references/pipeline-diagrams.md). Use actual source
material for entities, conditions, examples, model names, and outcomes; keep
family and version identifiers as supplied. Preserve meaningful controls and
comparisons even when simplifying the visual. Quote source text exactly when
used as a quotation; label excerpts and keep summaries unquoted.

For a more systematic or conventional diagram, use
[structured diagrams](references/structured-diagrams.md). Select a formal
notation only when the task or audience calls for it.

When useful graphics are available, use
[visual assets](references/visual-assets.md) to find and verify them or to work
in Canva. Use assets in service of the scientific content, with publication
rights and attribution checked for the intended output.

For data plots, use reproducible plotting code and choose a form appropriate
to the variables. Do not connect discrete categories by default. Show units,
denominators, and uncertainty when they are needed to interpret the data.
Identify material differences in populations or measurements in the figure or
caption, and explain reference lines or thresholds. Do not present a
descriptive proportion as an inferential result without the evidence for it.

## Deliver and review

Keep text, mathematical notation, charts, arrows, and other structure editable
or vector-based when feasible. Retain the source needed to revise the figure;
an illustration may be a separate bitmap component. If an editable PowerPoint
is requested, use native objects for text and structure and keep images as
independent components. Prefer a transparent canvas unless the task, output
format, or venue calls for another background.

Render the figure and inspect it at its intended final size. Check readability,
spacing, overlaps, clipping, connections, visual hierarchy, scientific
fidelity, and grayscale contrast. Review it as a reader of the paper: is the
main contribution understandable, and do the depicted comparisons and flow
match the evidence? Revise the specific problems found and re-render; do not
treat an automated check as a substitute for visual and scientific review.
Report unresolved issues or unavailable assets honestly.
