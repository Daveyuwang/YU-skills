Audio AI

Audio AI covers models that hear, speak and make sound. A voice assistant is a good example: one model turns your words into text, another works out the answer, and a third reads it aloud. Newer models do all of this in one network and can listen while they talk.

Each paper below has an arXiv link and one simple sentence. Results are the numbers the authors report, and they were not rerun here. Links were checked against arXiv on November 2, 2026.

The 8 branches
Speech to text: Turning spoken words into written text. First paper: Whisper.

Text to speech: Turning written text into natural speech. First paper: F5-TTS.

Voice cloning and conversion: Copying a voice from a short clip, or changing who seems to be speaking. First paper: kNN-VC.

Music generation: Making music and sound effects from text. First paper: MusicGen.

Separating sounds: Pulling one sound out of a mix, such as vocals out of a song. First paper: Hybrid Transformer Demucs.

Understanding audio: Models that can label sound and answer questions about it. First paper: CLAP.

Real-time voice models: Models that listen and speak at the same time in a live conversation. First paper: Moshi.

Detecting fake voices: Telling real speech from AI speech, and marking AI audio so it can be found later. First paper: Does Audio Deepfake Detection Generalize?

Words you will see
ASR (automatic speech recognition): speech to text.

TTS: text to speech.

Spectrogram: a picture of sound that shows how loud each pitch is at each moment.

Vocoder: a model that turns a spectrogram back into a sound wave.

Audio codec: a model that squeezes sound into a small set of numbers or tokens, then rebuilds it.

Flow matching: training a model to move random noise step by step until it becomes real data.

Zero-shot voice cloning: copying a new voice from a short clip with no extra training on that voice.

Voice conversion: keeping the words but changing who seems to be saying them.

Full duplex: listening and speaking at the same time, like a person on a phone call.

Large audio language model (LALM or audio LLM): a large language model that can also take sound as input.

WER (word error rate): the share of words transcribed wrongly, so lower is better.

EER (equal error rate): a fake-voice detector's error rate at the point where false alarms and misses are equal, so lower is better.

Watermark: a hidden signal added to audio so that a detector can recognize it later.

Sparse autoencoder (SAE): a tool that splits a model's internal numbers into separate features people can inspect.

GRPO: a reinforcement learning method often used to improve a model after normal training, using rewards.

Speech to text
Papers that shaped the field
Listen, Attend and Spell (Aug 2015): One network listens to speech and writes out characters using attention.

Deep Speech 2 (Dec 2015): One network trained from audio to text that works for both English and Mandarin.

SpecAugment (Apr 2019): Hiding parts of the spectrogram during training makes speech recognition more accurate.

Conformer (May 2020): Adds convolution to a transformer so the model catches both nearby and distant patterns in speech.

wav2vec 2.0 (Jun 2020): Learns speech features from raw audio without labels, so far less labeled data is needed.

WavLM (Oct 2021): A self-supervised speech model built to handle many speech tasks, including telling speakers apart.

Whisper (Dec 2022): Trained on 680,000 hours of internet audio with transcripts, and works across many languages.

Zipformer (Oct 2023): A speech encoder that is faster, uses less memory and performs better than Conformer.

Moonshine (Oct 2024): Speech recognition models built for live transcription and voice commands.

Open models
Canary-1B-v2 and Parakeet (Sep 2025): Canary is reported to beat Whisper-large-v3 on English at 10 times the speed, and Parakeet covers the same 25 languages with 600M parameters.

Qwen3-ASR (Jan 2026): Two all-in-one speech recognition models released under the Apache 2.0 license.

VibeVoice-ASR (Jan 2026): Handles language switching with no language setting, and accepts your own context to get special terms right.

VibeVoice-ASR-Streaming (Sep 2026): Writes who said what as speech arrives, with no separate step for identifying speakers.

Is the score real?
Towards Quantifying Benchmark Optimization (Aug 2026): Finds that top models behave differently on benchmarks in ways that can inflate scores without better everyday transcription.

Preference-ASR (Jun 2026): A test set with instructions for how to write numbers, filler words, names and casing, where rankings shift by preference.

Text-Only LLM Correctors Break Correct Transcripts (Oct 2026): LLMs that fix transcripts also break correct text, and a single error score hides this.

When Audio Separation Hurts Zero-Shot ASR (Mar 2026): Cleaning audio with SAM-Audio before Whisper can lower accuracy even when the audio quality improves.

When models make up words
Whisper Hallucination Detection and Mitigation (Jun 2026): Steering with a sparse autoencoder cuts made-up text on non-speech audio from 72.63% to 14.11% for Whisper small.

Probing and Mitigating Hallucinations (Oct 2026): Traces made-up text to attention that leans too much on text tokens, then reduces it with a new model called AudioSLM.

Faster, smaller and better
DiaScriber (Aug 2026): One speech LLM that writes down what was said and who said it in multi-speaker audio.

Look Less, Hear Better (Sep 2026): Rewarding both accuracy and speed cuts streaming errors by 30.8% at an 80 ms delay with no change to the model's design.

Learning New Words from Unlabeled Test Data (Sep 2026): Picks up new words from unlabeled audio, cutting character errors on recurring unknown words by up to 14.97% on LibriSpeech.

Oído (Oct 2026): Speech recognition that is not limited to a fixed word list, running on a $5 microcontroller.

Text to speech
Papers that shaped the field
WaveNet (Sep 2016): Generates sound one sample at a time, which made machine speech sound far more natural.

Tacotron 2 (Dec 2017): Turns text into a spectrogram, then uses WaveNet to turn that into sound.

FastSpeech 2 (Jun 2020): Generates speech all at once instead of frame by frame, and predicts pitch, energy and duration.

VITS (Jun 2021): Goes straight from text to sound in one model, with no separate vocoder.

VALL-E (Jan 2023): Treats speech as a sequence of codec tokens, so a language model can generate it.

StyleTTS 2 (Jun 2023): Uses style diffusion and adversarial training to aim for human-level speech.

Voicebox (Jun 2023): Uses flow matching to generate speech and to fill in parts of existing speech.

Matcha-TTS (Sep 2023): Uses flow matching to make speech quickly with little memory.

Natural language guidance of text-to-speech (Feb 2024): Controls the speaker and recording style with plain written descriptions.

Seed-TTS (Jun 2024): A family of autoregressive models whose speech the authors call virtually indistinguishable from human speech.

XTTS (Jun 2024): Zero-shot voice cloning that reaches many languages, including ones with little training data.

F5-TTS (Oct 2024): Generates speech with flow matching and no duration model, text encoder or phoneme alignment.

Fish-Speech (Nov 2024): Uses large language models to handle complex language features across many languages.

Vocoders: from spectrogram to sound
HiFi-GAN (Oct 2020): A fast vocoder trained with adversarial networks that makes high-quality speech.

BigVGAN (Jun 2022): A vocoder that also works on unseen speakers, languages, singing voices and music.

Vocos (Jun 2023): Predicts sound in the frequency domain, which makes it much faster than time-domain vocoders.

Real-Time Streaming Mel Vocoding (Sep 2025): A flow matching vocoder that runs live on a consumer laptop GPU with 48 ms of total delay.

Open models
VibeVoice (Aug 2025): Generates up to 90 minutes of speech with up to 4 speakers.

Qwen3-TTS (Jan 2026): A controllable family for many languages, released with its tokenizers under Apache 2.0.

Fish Audio S2 (Mar 2026): Open multi-speaker text to speech with under 100 ms before the first audio.

MOSS-TTS (Mar 2026): Supports zero-shot cloning, control of duration and pronunciation, and stable long speech.

Chatterbox-Flash (May 2026): Fine-tunes an existing voice cloning model so that it can stream and generate speech faster.

What happens inside the model
Tracing a Sparse Emotion-Control Circuit (Oct 2026): Traces a small route inside an LLM-based model that carries emotion from the reference clip to the speech.

Discovering Interpretable Acoustic Features (Oct 2026): Finds 684 validated sparse autoencoder features in Qwen3-TTS, covering speakers, speaking style and recording conditions.

How to test and improve it
Training a Compact, Strong, and Realistic TTS Model (Oct 2026): Trains in three stages, ending with reinforcement learning guided by a realism critic.

Listen, Critique, and Refine (Sep 2026): The model hears its own speech, critiques it and tries again, improving 7.15% on InstructTTSEval.

Beyond the Benchmark (Oct 2026): Finds that Qwen3-TTS leads on being understood and on pitch register but trails on prosody, and that metrics rank systems differently from listeners.

Measuring the Verifier's Floor (Oct 2026): Warns that checking speech with a recognizer can blame the speech model for the recognizer's own rare-word mistakes.

What Makes a Good Audio Prompt? (Oct 2026): Picks the best reference clip without generating anything, cutting word errors by a third for Qwen3-TTS.

Faster, smaller and longer
DriftTTS (Oct 2026): Matches the multi-step quality of Matcha-TTS with a single decoder call.

Paradee (Oct 2026): Shrinks Kokoro-82M into a single-voice model with 8.07M parameters.

ImageTTS (Oct 2026): Reuses pretrained image diffusion models to make speech.

Tacit-TTS (Sep 2026): Clones voices with no transcript of the reference clip, even from babble or other languages.

Voice cloning and conversion
Papers that shaped the field
Neural Voice Cloning with a Few Samples (Feb 2018): Copies a new voice from only a few recordings.

SV2TTS (Jun 2018): A separately trained speaker model lets text to speech use voices it never saw.

AUTOVC (May 2019): An autoencoder that the authors say was the first to do zero-shot voice conversion.

kNN-VC (May 2023): Changes a voice by swapping each moment of speech for the closest match from the target speaker.

OpenVoice (Dec 2023): Copies a voice from a short clip and generates speech in multiple languages.

Seed-VC (Nov 2024): Zero-shot voice conversion with diffusion transformers, aimed at stopping the original voice from leaking through.

Recent models
X-Voice (May 2026): A 0.4B model that clones voices across 30 languages.

Cross-Lingual F5-TTS 2 (Sep 2026): Reaches higher speaker similarity than F5-TTS while staying understandable across languages.

MeanVC 2 (Jun 2026): Streaming voice conversion that cuts delay from 211 ms to 110 ms.

One-Step Voice Conversion (Sep 2026): A 13M-parameter model that learns what kNN-VC does and converts in one step.

SoulX-Singer (Feb 2026): Zero-shot singing voice generation with a benchmark for testing it.

Synthetic Singers (Jan 2026): A review of deep learning for singing voice generation.

Limits, risks and protection
Voice "Cloning" is Style Transfer (May 2026): Shows that cloning makes voices more alike, with less variation in accent and speaking rate.

Decodability Is Not Controllability (Oct 2026): Being able to read a speaker's traits inside a model does not mean you can control them.

Expose Your Disguise (Jul 2026): Recovers who really spoke from converted speech, with accuracy as high as 90.99%.

Silence-the-Mimic (Sep 2026): Adds hard-to-hear changes to audio to block cloning, up to 45.3 times faster than earlier methods.

Five questions for inspiration
Can you stop Whisper tiny from making up words?: Whisper can write fluent text for audio with no speech. Collect noise clips, count the made-up words, and test whether a simple fix lowers the count without hurting real speech.

Does cleaning audio help or hurt transcription on your own recordings?: Audio that sounds cleaner can be harder to transcribe. Record noisy clips, run Whisper before and after cleaning, and compare word errors.

Which short clip gives the best voice clone?: The reference clip changes the result a lot. Clone one voice from clips with different lengths and noise levels, then compare word errors and speaker similarity.

Can a small detector catch a voice model it never saw?: Detectors often fail on new generators. Train a small one on a few generators, then test it on speech from a newer model such as Kokoro or F5-TTS.

Does a watermark survive everyday audio processing?: Speech cleaning can destroy watermarks. Add AudioSeal to some clips, run compression and cleaning, and measure how often the detector still finds it.
